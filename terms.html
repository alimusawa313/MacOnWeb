2. What MacOn is

MacOn comes in three parts, and they are not all licensed the same way:

  • The macon CLI and MaconKit. Free and open source under the MIT licence. That licence governs them, and nothing in this document takes away a right the MIT licence gives you.
  • The Mac app. Free to download and use. It runs the local CI, and it hosts the server the companion connects to.
  • The iPhone and iPad companion. Free to download, with an optional in app purchase called MacOn Pro. This is the only part that ever asks for money.

What it does: it watches a Git repository and runs build pipelines on your Mac, and it lets a paired phone or iPad monitor those builds, see and control the Mac's screen, wake and unlock the Mac, run automations, edit code, and talk to AI models you choose.

6. Free, Pro, and the limits

MacOn's free tier is generous on purpose. On your own network the screen, control and CompactOS are unlimited, and build monitoring, live logs, running and cancelling builds and push notifications are unlimited from anywhere.

Reaching your Mac from outside your own network is metered on the free tier at 40 minutes a day, which resets at midnight. When it runs out the session ends cleanly, with a warning at five minutes and one minute remaining. Time spent on your own network never counts against it, and build monitoring never counts against it anywhere.

MacOn Pro removes that limit and adds the remote unlock, the privacy curtain, higher stream quality, the AI agent that drives the Mac, the full code workspace, unlimited Deck keys and Flows, every theme, and unlimited paired Macs. The pricing page lists the current split line by line.

We may change what the free tier includes in future versions of the app, and we may change prices. We will not take away a feature you have already paid for. A price change never applies to a purchase you have already made, and for a subscription Apple asks for your consent before charging a higher renewal.

9. Services you may switch on

MacOn works with several outside services. Each one is optional, each one is off until you turn it on, and each one has its own terms and its own privacy policy that apply to your use of it:

  • Cloudflare quick tunnel, if you use MacOn's built in way to reach your Mac from outside your network. Your traffic passes through Cloudflare's network on the way.
  • iCloud, if you let the Mac publish a beacon so your devices can find it again after its address changes.
  • AI providers. Local models through Ollama never leave your Mac. If you add your own key for Anthropic, OpenAI, Google or any OpenAI compatible endpoint, your prompts and, for the agent, screenshots of your Mac go to that provider under their policy, not ours.
  • Bitbucket or GitHub, for the repositories you ask MacOn to watch, using credentials you supply.
  • Apple Push Notification service, using a push key you supply, with your own Mac acting as the provider.

We do not control these services and we are not responsible for them. If one of them changes or goes away, the MacOn feature that depends on it may stop working.
